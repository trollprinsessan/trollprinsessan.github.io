## 1. The two repos, and why there are two

**This one — the design site.**
`~/norrsken100`, remote `trollprinsessan/trollprinsessan.github.io`, branch
`main`. This is where Nina art-directs. Every push to `main` runs a GitHub
Action (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`) that builds and deploys to
https://trollprinsessan.github.io. That URL is the working preview, not the
real home.

**The real home — the pages monorepo.**
`~/norrsken-pages`, remote `norrsken-foundation/norrsken-pages`, package
`packages/impact100`. It serves https://pages.norrsken.org/100, which
www.norrsken.org embeds in an iframe. Felix and Cristian own it.

The flow is: design here → port into `packages/impact100` → PR → they merge.
Three PRs have landed already (#112 the list view, #113 the whole page, #114
making /100 the canonical, indexable home), so **pages.norrsken.org/100 is
already live with the full 2026 page**. Anything you change here has to be
ported across before it reaches the public site.

**Porting, practically.** The React lives in
`packages/impact100/src/Impact100View.tsx` — the same component as
`components/archive.tsx` here, adapted to their data shape. The CSS ships as
one inlined string, `ARCHIVE_CSS` in `packages/impact100/src/archive.css.ts`,
generated from `app/globals.css` by scoping every selector under `.nk100`. Two
things to know: the port has one pages-only rule (`.nk100 { overflow-x: clip;
}`) that must survive every regeneration, and the file is a template literal,
so backslashes, backticks and `${` need escaping. Their view reads campaign
tags from Backstage (`c.tags`), not from our hardcoded lists — so tags added
here do not appear there until Backstage has them.

Run both locally with the Browser pane rather than a terminal: `.claude/launch.json`
defines `norrsken100` (port 3000) and `norrsken-pages` (port 3939).

## 2. Architecture

Next.js 15, App Router, React 19, TypeScript. No CSS framework, no component
library, no animation library. Two files carry almost everything:

- **`components/archive.tsx`** (~1,770 lines) — the list. Grid view, index
  view, the company modal, the index panel, the phone sheet, the dock
  (filter / search / shuffle / view switch), URL state, keyboard shortcuts.
- **`app/globals.css`** (~6,170 lines) — all styling, heavily commented with
  the reasoning behind each decision. Read the comments before changing a
  rule; most of them record something that was tried and rejected.

Around those:

- `app/page.tsx` — the one page: hero film → manifest → mark → archive →
  The Latest → Good News → partners → FAQ → footer.
- `components/modules/*` — one file per section (`hero.tsx`, `manifest.tsx`,
  `latest.tsx`, `partners.tsx`, `faq.tsx`, `footer.tsx`, `hero-menu.tsx`,
  `hero-osd.tsx`). These are the sections Nina wants pushed further.
- `components/loader.tsx` — the intro: the index's own pictures fill the
  screen row by row, the wordmark is left behind. Only plays at the front
  door (a query, a hash or a back/forward visit skips it — decided by a
  script in `app/layout.tsx` before first paint).
- `lib/art-direction.ts` — which picture belongs to which company, which are
  line drawings / cutouts / photos / gifs, and the two campaign lists.
- `lib/composition.ts` — the grid's order. Not alphabetical: twelve named
  companies open it, then the rest are spread so no two neighbours share a
  campaign or a picture type. The gallery is composed, not sorted.
- `lib/companies.json` — 429 records, the whole archive, 100 of them 2026.

**Layout system.** Twelve tracks, `var(--pad)` and `var(--gutter)`. The index
rows, the dock and the grid all sit on the same tracks — that alignment is
the design. The dock takes the middle eight (3–10). The index rows are name
1–2, one-liner 3–6, campaign 7–8, sector 9–11, country 12.

**Type.** Arial only, 13/15 on phones and 14/16 on desktop, 11px for tags.
Black, white, one grey. No serifs, no rounded corners, no shadows.

**Motion.** Functional or absent. No bounce, no slide-in, no staggered lists.
The one deliberate exception is the intro's film-paint: uneven bands from the
top down, hard cuts (`@keyframes film-paint`). If you need motion, copy that
rhythm rather than inventing another.

## 3. Where the content comes from

**Company data.** `lib/companies.json`, generated by
`node scripts/transform-csv.mjs <export.csv>`. The current 2026 text came from
`~/Downloads/2026 List-Website data final.csv` (saved 14 Sep), imported 15 Sep.
There is a newer sheet, `2026 List-Content production.csv` (15 Sep, 21:58):
the company text in it is word-for-word identical, but it adds the campaign
tags, Image Type, SDG, business model, and who does due diligence on which
company. Two names changed in it and are still wrong on the site: **R3Robotics
→ R3 Robotics**, and **Sava Technology → Sava**.

**Campaign tags.** Hardcoded in `lib/art-direction.ts` as `ELECTRO_UNION` (28
companies) and `PROMPT_WHAT_MATTERS` (17), read through `cohortsFor(slug)`.
They come from the **Image Type** column of the content-production sheet —
that column is the decider, not the campaign copy columns, which are filled in
for companies that are not in the campaign. Matter is in Electro Union
provisionally while its picture is decided. NEU Battery Materials and NuCicer
have no campaign. On the pages side these will come from Backstage instead.

**Pictures.** `public/n100/` (104 files) with 320px thumbs in
`public/n100/thumb/`. Originals live in Google Drive:
`5. MarComm/12_Impact100/Norrsken100 2026/Norrsken100 Images`. The processing
is a Pillow script run ad hoc, not committed: cutouts cropped to their alpha
box and laid on 1600×1067 at 92%; photos capped at 1600px, webp q85; gifs
cropped, 800×533, every other frame, 127 colours. 97 of the 100 companies have
a picture — Djamo, NEU Battery Materials and Unobravo do not.

**The pages side pulls live data** from
`https://backstage.norrsken.org/api/public/impact100` (429 companies, 100 for
2026). Backstage currently has almost no images and no tags, which is why this
repo carries its own art direction.

## 5. How Nina works (worth knowing before you touch anything)

- References: SSENSE, Acne, 032c, Giga Design Studio. Editorial, austere,
  typography-led. Never "clean and modern".
- Reduction over addition. If you are adding something, it probably should go.
- Never change beyond what was asked. If you touch a neighbouring element,
  say so.
- Show, don't describe. Build it and put it on the preview.
- Verify before saying it works: which port, which commit, what you actually
  clicked. "It compiles" is not verification.

## 6. Worries before go-live

Roughly in the order I would deal with them.

1. **Two sources of truth.** Everything visual here — art direction,
   composition, campaign tags — is hardcoded in this repo, while the pages
   side reads Backstage. Every design decision therefore has to be ported by
   hand, and the campaign tags cannot cross at all until Backstage carries
   them. Decide who owns what before launch, or the two will drift.
2. **The copy has never been checked by the team.** There is a marcom review
   planned — every company's copy, picture, and website link, with feedback
   going into an Airtable column. It has not started. The text on the site is
   the 14 Sep export.
3. **Three companies have no picture** (Djamo, NEU Battery Materials,
   Unobravo), and Matter's image type is still undecided.
4. **The counts in the manifest are hand-written.** The Electro Union tab says
   28 builders because that is what the tags currently give. If Matter moves,
   the copy has to move with it. Nothing checks this.
5. **Three one-liners still carry two alternative versions** joined by " / "
   (Mazama, Mantle8, Vind AI). They overflow the index column and read as a
   mistake. Someone has to choose.
6. **`archive.tsx` and `globals.css` are very large.** Both are coherent but
   long. Before adding a section, read the comments around what you are
   touching — most rules exist because something else broke.
7. **The pages port is a manual pipeline.** The CSS is regenerated by scoping
   selectors under `.nk100`, and one pages-only rule has to be re-inserted
   every time. It is easy to lose silently. It deserves a script in the repo.
8. **Browser support of newer CSS.** The panel's slot heights use `lh` units
   and the layout leans on `dvh` and subgrid. Fine in current Chrome, Safari
   and Firefox; worth a check on whatever the Norrsken site's analytics say
   people actually use.
9. **Verification in the preview pane is unreliable.** When the pane is
   hidden, the browser pauses animation frames, transitions and some scroll
   events, so anything motion- or scroll-driven has to be checked with the
   pane open, or measured rather than watched.

## 7. Where to start on the grid

The grid is the part Nina looks at most, and the part with the least art
direction left in it. Things worth exploring, none of them decided:

- The card is uniform: picture, name, one-liner, tags. The list of 100 reads
  evenly. Some editorial variation in size or emphasis — one big, two small,
  the way a contents page works — is the obvious move, and the composition
  logic in `lib/composition.ts` already knows which companies sit where.
- The tags are boxed on cards but plain everywhere else. Pick one.
- Density: 6 across on desktop, 2 on a phone, switchable to 8 and 12. The
  tighter settings drop the text and become a contact sheet; they are the
  least resolved of the three.
- The Latest, Good News, partners and FAQ are all further from the list's
  level of finish than the list is.
